1. Aircraft systems: the hydraulic system

The A320 hydraulic system, up to Green

Narration

Welcome to part three, the hydraulic system of the A320, chapter 29. The exam scope stops after the Green system, so we cover the architecture shared by the three systems, the controls and indications, and then follow the Green system from its reservoir to its users; the detailed Yellow and Blue pages are out.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic)

2. Three independent systems at 3000 psi

1 · Architecture
  • GREEN (main): engine-driven pump on ENG 1; most users, biggest reservoir
  • YELLOW ('alternate'): EDP on ENG 2 + electric pump + hand pump
  • BLUE (emergency): electric pump + RAT
  • All at 3000 psi; EDPs are variable displacement
  • Fully independent: own reservoir, pumps, pipes and users; PTU links Green and Yellow mechanically (Δp 500 psi)
  • All three reservoirs are filled from the GREEN service panel
Narration

The A320 has three independent hydraulic systems, named by colour. Green works as the main system: it has a pump driven by engine 1, the most users and the biggest reservoir. Yellow behaves as an alternate system: an engine-driven pump on engine 2, plus an electric pump and a hand pump. Blue is the emergency system, with an electric pump and the ram air turbine; Airbus does not use these labels, but compared with other aircraft those are their roles. All three work at 3000 psi, and the engine-driven pumps are of the variable displacement type. Each system has its own reservoir, pumps, pipes and users, so a leak in one cannot drain another; the only link is the power transfer unit between Green and Yellow, which is mechanical and acts at a pressure difference of 500 psi. And a detail the quiz tests: all three reservoirs are filled from the service panel of the Green system.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 2; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.1

3. Users and redundancy

1 · Architecture
  • GREEN: landing gear and doors, nose-wheel steering, normal brakes, reverser ENG 1, flaps/slats motors, THS, rudder, yaw damper, spoilers, aileron, elevator
  • YELLOW: reverser ENG 2, flaps, cargo doors, alternate brakes, rudder, THS, spoilers, elevator
  • BLUE: emergency generator (CSM/G), slats, rudder, spoilers, aileron, elevator
  • Ailerons: two servos each, BLUE + GREEN
  • Elevators: left GREEN + BLUE, right BLUE + YELLOW → pitch survives any single loss
Narration

This deck page lists the users of each system, read upwards from each reservoir. Green, the biggest, supplies the landing gear and its doors, the nose-wheel steering, the normal brakes, the reverser of engine 1, the flap and slat motors, the trimmable horizontal stabiliser, the rudder, a yaw damper and many flight control servos. Yellow supplies the reverser of engine 2, the flaps, the cargo doors, the alternate brakes and its share of flight controls. Blue supplies the constant speed motor generator, which is the emergency electrical generator, the slats and the rest of its flight control servos. The flight controls show the redundancy philosophy: each aileron has two servo actuators, one Blue and one Green. And the elevators: on the left one servo is Green and the other Blue, on the right one Blue and one Yellow, so pitch control survives the loss of any single system; fail safe, as the notes say.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 7; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.1, §2.6

4. Pumps: engine-driven, electric and manual

2 · Power sources
  • EDP (Green ENG 1, Yellow ENG 2) on the accessory gearbox: turns whenever the engine turns
  • Variable displacement: pistons in a rotating barrel on an inclined swashplate; tilt set by a solenoid → 3000 psi; plate neutral = no pressure (pushbutton OFF)
  • Fed by the reservoir at 50 psi (no cavitation); lubricated by its own fluid via the case-drain filter
  • Electric pumps (AC): Yellow (backup, cargo doors) and Blue (main Blue pump), 50 psi in → 3000 psi out
  • MEL: Blue electric pump NO GO; Yellow electric pump category C (10 days); identical, can be swapped
  • Hand pump (Yellow): cargo doors with no electrical power
Narration

Let us look at the power sources. The engine-driven pumps, Green on engine 1 and Yellow on engine 2, sit on the accessory gearbox, so they turn whenever the engine turns, even when they are not delivering pressure. They are variable displacement pumps: pistons in a rotating barrel, like the cylinder of a revolver, rest on a plate whose tilt is set by a solenoid valve; tilted, the pistons stroke and deliver fluid at 3000 psi, and with the plate neutral there is no flow, which is the state with the pushbutton at OFF. They are fed from the reservoir at 50 psi so that they never cavitate, and the same fluid lubricates and cools the pump and returns through the case-drain filter. The alternating current electric pumps are a backup on Yellow, also used for the cargo doors, and the main pump of the Blue system; they take fluid at 50 psi and deliver 3000. The minimum equipment list treats them differently: without the Blue electric pump the aircraft cannot fly, while the Yellow one is a category C item, 10 days to repair; since they are identical, a failed Blue pump can be swapped with the Yellow one. Finally Yellow has a hand pump, to operate the cargo doors when there is no electrical power.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, pp. 2, 8; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.1.2–2.1.4

5. RAT, accumulators and the PTU

2 · Power sources
  • RAT (Blue): two-blade turbine, left ventral fuselage; powers Blue + electrics via the CSM/G [< 4.5 s, 4800–6400 rpm: RAT page, beyond scope]
  • RAT deployment: automatic on loss of electrical power, or guarded RAT MAN ON [spring-pack detail: later RAT page, beyond scope]
  • Accumulators: nitrogen pre-charge (Green: 1885 psi at 20 °C, 1 l) damp pressure drops when users demand flow
  • PTU: hydraulic motor + pump on one shaft between GREEN and YELLOW; runs when Δp > 500 psi with the PTU pushbutton in AUTO
  • PTU inhibited during engine start and cargo-door operation with the Yellow electric pump

Narration

Three more elements complete the power side. The ram air turbine is the emergency source of the Blue system: a two-blade turbine stowed in the left ventral fuselage whose variable-pitch blades hold a constant speed whatever the airspeed, the timing and rpm figures being on a later page beyond the exam scope, driving a Blue pump and, through the constant speed motor generator, electrical power. It is deployed by a compressed spring pack, released when either of two solenoid valves is energised, automatically on a loss of electrical power, or manually with the guarded RAT MAN ON pushbutton. Accumulators hold fluid against a nitrogen pre-charge, 1885 psi at 20 degrees in the one-litre Green accumulator, so that when a user suddenly demands flow, the pressure drop is absorbed before the pump reacts. The power transfer unit is a hydraulic motor and a pump on a common shaft, one on the Green side and one on the Yellow side: with its pushbutton in AUTO, when one system is more than 500 psi below the other, the healthy system drives the motor, which turns the pump of the weak system, transferring power but never fluid. It is inhibited during engine start and when the cargo doors are operated with the Yellow electric pump, so that an aircraft on the ground does not start transferring power unexpectedly.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, pp. 2, 8, RAT page; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.1.5–2.1.7, §2.7.4

6. Reservoir pressurisation: the pneumatic link

2 · Power sources
  • Each reservoir pressurised to 50 psi (3.5 bar) so the pumps are always primed
  • Normal source: HP compressor stage 8 of ENGINE 1
  • Backup: the pneumatic manifold if engine 1 is lost; on the ground: a ground supply
  • Reservoirs are sealed: they keep their pressure after a failure or engine shut-down
  • Warnings: LO AIR PRESS below 22 psi (Green, Yellow), 30 psi (Blue)
50 psireservoir pressurestage 8HP compressor, ENG 122 psilow-air warning, Green
Narration

The reservoirs are pressurised with air, and that is where the two systems of this exam meet. Each reservoir is pressurised to 50 psi, 3.5 bar, so that the pumps are always primed and never cavitate. The air normally comes from stage 8 of the high pressure compressor of engine 1. If engine 1 is lost, the pneumatic system supplies it, and on the ground a ground supply can be used. The reservoirs are sealed, so they keep their pressure after a pressurisation failure or an engine shut-down. If the air pressure falls below 22 psi in Green or Yellow, or 30 psi in Blue, the ECAM shows a low air pressure warning.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, reservoir pressurisation page; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.1.1, §2.7

7. Overhead panel 40VU and the leak-measurement panel

3 · Controls and indication
  • ENG 1 PUMP (Green), ENG 2 PUMP (Yellow): dark = pressurising; white OFF = pump turning, no pressure; amber FAULT
  • Yellow ELEC PUMP: blue ON when selected; amber FAULT
  • Blue ELEC PUMP in AUTO: runs with power AND (an engine running, or in flight, or BLUE PUMP OVRD ON) [Blue page: beyond scope]
  • PTU pushbutton: AUTO / OFF, amber FAULT
  • RAT MAN ON under a red guard
  • Second panel: BLUE PUMP OVRD (maintenance) + LEAK MEASUREMENT VALVES G, B, Y
Narration

The crew controls the hydraulics from overhead panel 40VU, next to the air conditioning and pneumatic panels. The engine pump pushbuttons, ENG 1 PUMP for Green and ENG 2 PUMP for Yellow, are dark when the pump is pressurising; a white OFF means the pump is turning with the engine but not delivering pressure, and an amber FAULT means a failure. The Yellow electric pump shows a blue ON when it has been switched on, a non-normal state that must be cancelled later, and amber FAULT on failure. The Blue electric pump pushbutton sits in AUTO, and, with electrical power available, the pump runs when an engine is running, or in flight, which the system knows from the nose-gear shock absorber being extended, or when the BLUE PUMP OVERRIDE is selected. The PTU pushbutton arms the power transfer unit in AUTO, with its own amber FAULT. The RAT MAN ON pushbutton is protected by a red guard. A second panel further aft holds four guarded pushbuttons: BLUE PUMP OVERRIDE, used by maintenance to run the Blue pump on the ground, and the three leak measurement valves, Green, Blue and Yellow.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 4 (Blue pump conditions on the Blue page); Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.3

8. The ECAM HYD page

3 · Controls and indication
  • System label and pressure: green when normal (3000 psi), amber when low
  • Pump symbol: in line + green = delivering; across + amber = OFF; amber LO = low pressure (< 1740 psi)
  • Reservoir: arrow at the box = normal level; lower + amber = low level; OVHT; LO AIR PRESS
  • Fire valve: in line green = open; across amber = fully closed
  • PTU and RAT: white = available; amber = transient; green in line = supplying
Narration

Pressing HYD on the ECAM control panel brings up this page on the lower display. Each system has its label and its pressure from a transmitter, green when normal, around 3000 psi, and amber when low. Each pump has a symbol: a square in line with the supply line and green when delivering pressure; across the line and amber when the pump is selected off; and an amber LO when it is selected on but the pressure switch after it reads less than 1740 psi. The reservoir shows its level with an arrow, green at the box when normal, lower and amber when low, plus an overheat indication when the returning fluid is too hot, which suggests heavy internal leakage, and LO AIR PRESS when the reservoir pressurisation falls. The fire valves of Green and Yellow are shown in line and green when open and across and amber when fully closed, and the PTU and RAT are white when available, amber while changing state, and green when supplying.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 5; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.4

10. Identifying hydraulic pipes

3 · Controls and indication
  • Supply and return lines; return lines usually larger
  • Hydraulic tape colour code tells hydraulics from fuel or air (pneumatic ducts are much thicker)
  • Arrows give the flow direction; black dots = pressure line
  • System band number: 1 Green, 2 Blue, 3 Yellow
  • Part number printed to find the pipe in the manual
Narration

In the aircraft, every pipe carries a coded band, and this slide shows the A320 hydraulic code. There are supply and return lines, and the return lines are usually of larger diameter. The colour combination identifies a hydraulic line, as opposed to fuel; pneumatic ducts are much thicker and easy to tell apart. Arrows show the direction of flow, and black dots mark a pressure line. A numbered band identifies the system: 1 is Green, 2 is Blue and 3 is Yellow. And a part number lets the mechanic find exactly that pipe in the manuals.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 6; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.5

11. Leak-measurement valves and internal-leak tests

3 · Controls and indication
  • Each system: a leak-measurement valve isolates its PRIMARY FLIGHT CONTROL users from the others
  • Normally open: flight controls are permanently fed through it
  • Return flow = demand flow + permanent internal leakage (users in neutral still leak)
  • Test: external power + flowmeter; close sections one by one [test detail: later pages, beyond scope]
  • Too much leakage → heating (OVHT) and degraded operation; find the section, disconnect the suspect servo, re-test
Narration

The leak measurement valves deserve their own slide because the quiz uses them. In each system, a leak measurement valve isolates the group of primary flight control users from the rest of the users of that system. Normally it is open, so the flight controls are permanently supplied through it; it closes for the leak test, and on Yellow also automatically when the cargo doors are operated with the electric pump, so that no surface can move. Leaks are tested because the return flow equals the demand plus the permanent internal leakage of every component, which flows even when the users are in neutral. The test uses an external hydraulic power unit with a flowmeter; the internal leakage is read on the flowmeter, and closing the valves section by section, right wing, left wing, tail and centre, locates the guilty group by comparison with the allowed values. Excess leakage heats the fluid and degrades the system; when a servo control is suspected, it is disconnected and the section is re-tested to confirm it.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, pp. 3, leak-test page; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.2, §2.3

12. Green system: from the reservoir to the pump

4 · The Green system
  • Reservoir: 18 l max (14 l normal); LOW LEVEL at 3 l; air at 50 psi, LO AIR PRESS at 22 psi
  • Fills ALL three systems from here (filling selector valve: Green / Yellow / Blue)
  • Fire valve in the LH pylon, electric motor; closed by ENG 1 FIRE pushbutton
  • EDP: 3000 psi, self-regulating, variable displacement; case-drain filter (clogged at 87 psi Δp) back to the reservoir
  • Pump pressure switch: LO below 1740 psi; normal-pressure line also feeds the ENG 1 reverser
Narration

Now we follow the fluid through the Green system, which is the last part of the exam scope. The reservoir holds 18 litres at most, about 14 in normal operation, with a low level warning at 3 litres; it is pressurised to 50 psi with a low air pressure warning at 22 psi. The filling equipment of this system serves all three: a selector valve chooses whether the hand pump fills the Green, Yellow or Blue reservoir. From the reservoir, the fluid passes the fire valve, located in the left pylon and driven by an electric motor; when the crew pushes the ENG 1 FIRE pushbutton, it closes and isolates the Green fluid from the burning engine. Then comes the engine-driven pump, 3000 psi, self-regulating and of variable displacement, whose case-drain flow returns to the reservoir through its own filter, with a clogging indication at a pressure difference of 87 psi, not to be confused with the 3000 psi system pressure; a pressure switch after it triggers the LO indication below 1740 psi, and the line also feeds the reverser of engine 1.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 8 (Green system); Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.7

13. Green system: the HP manifold

4 · The Green system
  • HP filter with clogging indicator (87 psi Δp)
  • Check valve; sample port valve (fluid sampling, contamination analysis)
  • Pressure transducer → ELAC and ECAM (displayed pressure)
  • Pressure switch → ELAC, SEC, FAC, BSCU, ECAM: trips at 1450 psi
  • Relief valve: opens 3500 psi, closes 3200 psi
  • Leak-measurement valve; accumulator: N₂ 1885 psi at 20 °C, 1 l, steel with Kevlar wrap
Narration

The pump delivers into the high pressure manifold, a block that carries most of the Green system's small components, all with numbers worth knowing. The high pressure filter has a clogging indicator that pops out at a pressure difference of 87 psi, and is replaced when it does. There is a check valve, and a sample port valve to take fluid samples, which the laboratory analyses for water and particles that reveal worn components. A pressure transducer sends the pressure to the ELAC flight control computers and to the ECAM display. A pressure switch informs the ELAC, SEC and FAC flight computers, the brake and steering control unit, the BSCU, and the ECAM; it trips at 1450 psi. The relief valve opens at 3500 psi to protect the system and closes again at 3200 psi. Finally the leak measurement valve, and outside the manifold the accumulator: a one-litre steel cylinder wrapped in Kevlar with a nitrogen pre-charge of 1885 psi at 20 degrees.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 8 (HP manifold list), p. 11; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.7.2–2.7.3

14. Green system: PTU manifold, priority valve and return

4 · The Green system
  • Normal brake selector valve: cuts the supply to the normal brakes
  • Solenoid valve: cuts PTU operation (PTU pushbutton OFF)
  • Two check valves
  • PRIORITY valve: if pressure drops, cuts the heavy users (gear, doors, steering, slat/flap motors) so primary flight controls keep the pressure
  • Return manifold: temperature transmitter; return filter (29 psi)
Narration

The last block of the Green system is the PTU manifold, with its own dedicated set of valves. The normal brake selector valve supplies the normal braking system and cuts it off, putting it to return, when there is a problem. A solenoid valve, open with the PTU pushbutton in AUTO, cuts the operation of the power transfer unit when the pushbutton is off. Two check valves complete the PTU path. The priority valve, marked P, isolates the large-flow users, the landing gear and doors, the nose-wheel steering and the slat and flap motors, when the system pressure drops, so that all the pressure available goes to the primary flight controls and the aircraft stays controllable. The fluid returns through the return manifold, with a temperature transmitter and a return filter of 29 psi, back to the reservoir.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 9 (PTU and return manifolds); Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.7, §2.7.4

15. Green leak-measurement manifold: who is behind it

4 · The Green system
  • Leak-measurement valve feeds the Green PRIMARY FLIGHT CONTROL users, in three groups
  • TAIL: THS hydraulic motor, rudder, one yaw-damper actuator
  • LH WING: one aileron servo, spoilers 1 and 5
  • RH WING: the same on the right side
  • Spool valves of the manifold: normally closed, used only to check internal leakage section by section
Narration

Back on the Green schematic, the top block is the leak measurement manifold, and it is worth knowing who sits behind it. Through the leak measurement valve, it feeds the Green primary flight control users, split into three groups: tail, left wing and right wing. The tail group holds the hydraulic motor that drives the trimmable horizontal stabiliser screw, the rudder, and one of the yaw damper actuators that prevent Dutch roll. The left wing group holds one of the two servo actuators of the aileron and spoilers 1 and 5. The right wing group is the mirror image on the right. The spool valves drawn at the top are normally closed and are used only during maintenance, to check the internal leakage section by section.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 8; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.7

16. Where the Green components are

4 · The Green system
  • Almost everything in the MAIN LANDING GEAR bay: open the door and you see it
  • Reservoir: two welded cylinders, drain valve; air manifold with gauge, pressure switch, check valve; direct-reading quantity indicator
  • Low-level sensor: cylindrical capacitor, the fluid is the dielectric → signal at 3 l
Narration

Finally, where all of this lives. Almost every Green component is in the main landing gear bay: opening the gear door is enough to see the reservoir, the high pressure and PTU manifolds and the PTU. The reservoir is made of two welded metal cylinders, with a drain valve at the bottom, an air manifold carrying a pressure gauge, a pressure switch and a check valve, and a direct-reading quantity indicator on its side. Its low level sensor is a cylindrical capacitor in which the hydraulic fluid acts as the dielectric: as the quantity changes so does the capacitance, and at 3 litres it sends the low level signal.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 10; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.7.1

17. PTU manifold and the Green service panel

4 · The Green system
  • PTU manifold: priority valve, two check valves, PTU solenoid valve
  • Service panel behind the LH main gear door: filling selector valve (G / B / Y), quantity indicator with a range per system
  • Filling port + filter, FILLING hand pump (not the Yellow cargo-door hand pump): fills ALL three reservoirs
  • Ground connections: suction and pressure ports for a hydraulic ground cart ('mule') that bypasses the Green pump
Narration

This last Green page shows the PTU manifold on the left and the service panel on the right. On the PTU manifold you can recognise the priority valve, the two check valves and the solenoid valve that we met in the schematic. The service panel, behind the left main gear door, has the reservoir filling selector valve, to choose Green, Blue or Yellow, and a quantity indicator with a coloured range for each system. Its filling port with a filter and its filling hand pump, not to be confused with the Yellow hand pump of the cargo doors, are used to fill all three reservoirs, which is the fact behind statement b of the 2020 quiz. It also has the suction and pressure ports to connect a hydraulic ground cart, the mule, which bypasses the Green pump for maintenance and hangar tests.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistemas hidráulicos (ATA 29), course deck 2022, pp. 1–12, p. 12; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §2.1–2.7 (hydraulic), §2.7, §2.7.4

18. Exercise: Green-system failures, step by step

4 · The Green system

Self-test: think of your answer, then open it.

1 · Green EDP fails in flight, PTU in AUTO: first indication?

pump LO: the switch at the pump outlet reads < 1740 psi (amber on ECAM)

2 · … and then?

Δp > 500 psi → Yellow drives the PTU → Green held at ≈ 2500 psi: the 1450 psi switch is not reached, brakes and priority valve unaffected

3 · Same failure but PTU OFF/inhibited, or Green fluid lost: below 1450 psi?

manifold pressure switch → BSCU: normal brakes lost → alternate brakes on YELLOW

4 · … and as the pressure keeps falling?

PRIORITY valve cuts gear, doors, steering, slat/flap motors → primary flight controls keep what is left

5 · ENG 1 FIRE pushbutton pushed?

fire valve (LH pylon) closes → EDP loses its fluid → pump LO; the PTU can still pressurise Green from Yellow

6 · On ground, cargo doors with the Yellow ELEC PUMP?

PTU inhibited, Yellow leak-measurement valve closes, flap computer told not to move (together, not cause and effect)

Narration

To finish the Green system, let us trace failures the way an exam statement does; pause after each question. One: the Green engine-driven pump fails in flight with the PTU in AUTO; what is the first indication? The pressure switch at the pump outlet reads less than 1740 psi, and the ECAM shows the amber LO on the Green pump. And then? As soon as Green is 500 psi below Yellow, the Yellow system drives the PTU, which holds Green at about 2 500 psi, so the 1450 psi manifold switch is never reached, and the brakes and the priority valve are not affected. Three: the same failure, but with the PTU off or inhibited, or with the Green fluid lost, so the pressure falls below 1450 psi. The manifold pressure switch tells the brake and steering control unit, which loses the normal brakes and switches to the alternate brakes on the Yellow system. Four: and as the pressure keeps falling? The priority valve shuts off the heavy users, the landing gear and doors, the nose-wheel steering and the slat and flap motors, so that what is left goes to the primary flight controls. Five: the crew pushes the engine 1 fire pushbutton. The fire valve in the left pylon closes, the engine-driven pump loses its fluid and shows LO, and the PTU can still pressurise Green from Yellow, transferring power, not fluid. Six: on the ground, the cargo doors are operated with the Yellow electric pump. The PTU is inhibited, the Yellow leak measurement valve closes and the flap computer is told not to move the flaps; these happen together, which is the trap of statement b in quiz question 5, but none causes the others.

20. 2020 quiz, question 4: FALSE statements

5 · Exam questions
Problem. Circle the FALSE statements: a) the Green system feeds the nose-wheel steering. b) each hydraulic reservoir is filled from its own service panel. c) only the Blue system can be pressurised by the RAT. d) 'hydraulic transfer' between the Yellow and Green systems happens with the PTU pushbutton in AUTO and a Δp of at least 500 psi, except during engine start or cargo-door operation.
  1. a) TRUE: nose-wheel steering is a Green user (via the priority valve)
  2. b) FALSE: ALL reservoirs are filled from the GREEN service panel (selector valve)
  3. c) TRUE: the RAT drives the Blue pump (and the CSM/G)
  4. d) TRUE if read as POWER transfer (PTU, mechanical); FALSE if fluid (systems independent)
  5. FALSE: b (and d only if 'transfer' is read as fluid)
b false statement
Narration

Question 4 of the 2020 quiz, the false statements. Statement a is true: the nose-wheel steering is a Green user, fed through the priority valve. Statement b is false: there is a single filling point, the Green service panel, with a selector valve for the three reservoirs. Statement c is true: the ram air turbine drives a Blue pump and, through the constant speed motor generator, provides electrical power. Statement d speaks of hydraulic transfer: the conditions are right, AUTO, 500 psi, inhibited during engine start and cargo door operation, but what the PTU transfers is power, through its shaft, never fluid, since the systems are independent; read as power transfer, which is clearly what is meant, d is true. So the false statement is b, and if your examiner reads transfer literally as fluid, d as well: write one line saying the PTU transfers power, not fluid, and you are covered either way.

21. 2020 quiz, question 5: CORRECT statements

5 · Exam questions
Problem. Circle the CORRECT statements: a) arming the PTU closes the Green leak-measurement valve. b) the Green side of the PTU is inhibited if, while armed, the leak-measurement valve closes. c) with Green pressure below 1450 psi in flight, the normal brakes cannot be supplied. d) in flight, with Green 800 psi below Yellow and the PTU pushbutton pressed, the Green reservoir feeds the HP manifold.
  1. a) NO: the PTU and the leak-measurement valve are independent
  2. b) NO: PTU inhibition = engine start, cargo doors (Yellow elec pump), pushbutton OFF
  3. c) YES: the 1450 psi pressure switch tells the BSCU → normal brakes lost (alternate on Yellow)
  4. d) YES: Δp 800 > 500 → Yellow drives the PTU; its Green pump feeds Green HP from the Green reservoir
  5. correct: c, d (our reading; no official key)
c, d correct statements
Narration

Question 5 asks for the correct statements, and requires the logic of the PTU manifold. Statement a is wrong: arming the PTU has nothing to do with the leak measurement valve. Statement b is wrong too, and it is a trap: during cargo door operation with the Yellow electric pump, the PTU is inhibited and the Yellow leak measurement valve closes at the same time, but neither causes the other, and the PTU is never inhibited because a Green leak measurement valve closes. Statement c is correct: the Green pressure switch, which trips at 1450 psi, informs the brake and steering control unit, and with Green below that value the normal brakes are lost and the alternate brakes on Yellow take over. Statement d is correct: 800 psi is more than the 500 psi threshold, so the Yellow system drives the PTU motor, and the PTU pump on the Green side draws fluid from the Green reservoir and delivers it into the Green high pressure line. So the correct statements are c and d; there is no official key, so in the exam add one line of justification to each choice.
