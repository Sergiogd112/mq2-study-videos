1. Aircraft systems: the pneumatic system

The A320 pneumatic system (ATA 36)

Narration

Welcome to the first of three lectures for the aircraft systems exam of Vehicles Aeroespacials. The exam covers two systems of the Airbus A320: the whole pneumatic system, ATA chapter 36, and the hydraulic system, ATA chapter 29, up to and including the Green system. This part explains what the pneumatic system is for, where the air comes from, who uses it, and how the crew and the computers control it; part two goes inside every valve, and part three covers the hydraulics.

3. What the pneumatic system is for

1 · Introduction
  • Mission: deliver air from a SOURCE (engines, APU, ground cart) to the USERS at regulated pressure and temperature
  • A320 regulated bleed: 44 psi (PRV) and 200 °C (precooler), the system's reference values
  • Users: air conditioning and pressurisation (ATA 21), engine starting, wing, engine (and, generically, fin) anti-ice (ATA 30)
  • Pressurisation of hydraulic reservoirs (ATA 29) and of the potable water tank (ATA 38)
  • Motive power: thrust-reverser actuation, air-driven hydraulic pumps (ADP); aft cargo heating
Narration

The pneumatic system of an aircraft takes compressed air from one of its sources, the engines, the auxiliary power unit or a ground cart, and delivers it to the systems that need it. On the A320 the air is delivered regulated to 44 psi and about 200 degrees Celsius: those two numbers are the reference values of the whole system. The main user is the air conditioning and pressurisation system, chapter 21, then engine starting and the anti-ice of the wings and the engine intakes, chapter 30; the deck's generic list also mentions the vertical stabiliser. Bleed air also pressurises the hydraulic reservoirs, so that the pumps never cavitate, and the potable water tank, so that water reaches the galleys and toilets. Finally, it can provide motive power, for example to actuate thrust reversers or to drive air-driven hydraulic pumps, and on the A320 it also heats the aft cargo hold, which is needed to carry live animals.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 3–4; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1, §1.4

4. The three sources of compressed air

1 · Introduction
  • ENGINES: main source in flight; high stages give 50–410 psi and 180–540 °C (idle → cruise)
  • APU: gas turbine for electric + pneumatic power on the ground and, auxiliary, in flight up to 20 000 ft
  • GROUND CART: pneumatic power for maintenance and engine start, through a connector with a check valve
  • MEL: which items may be inoperative and for how long (deferred defects: B 3 days, C 10 days, D 120 days)
  • Conventions: odd numbers = left, even = right; the APU and ground cart feed the LEFT (side 1) of the crossbleed duct
50–410 psiengine bleed, idle → cruise180–540 °Cengine bleed temperature20 000 ftAPU bleed ceiling
Narration

There are three sources of compressed air. The engines are the main source in flight: from their high compressor stages the air comes at between 50 and 410 psi and between 180 and 540 degrees Celsius, from idle to cruise, far too hot and too variable to use directly, which is why it must be regulated. The auxiliary power unit, the APU, is a small gas turbine in the tail that supplies electrical and pneumatic power on the ground, for engine starting and air conditioning, and in flight as a backup, up to 20 000 feet. On the ground, a pneumatic cart can be connected through a quick-release access panel under the fuselage, onto a connector with a check valve that stops the air escaping. The notes stress that the APU is on the Minimum Equipment List, the document that says which items may be inoperative and for how long: deferred defects of category B, C and D allow 3, 10 and 120 days. Two drawing conventions: odd numbers are on the left and even numbers on the right, and both the APU and the ground connection feed the left side, side 1, of the crossbleed duct.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slide 5, A320 slides; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.1

5. A generic pneumatic system

1 · Introduction
  • Each engine: bleed valves (dashed ducts) → its own side of the manifold
  • Crossbleed (isolation) valve splits the manifold into left and right
  • APU joins one side through its bleed valve and a check valve
  • Users hang off the manifold: packs, anti-ice, starters, pressurisation
  • Same philosophy on every airliner: any source can feed any user, with isolation for failures
Narration

This is the generic layout from the deck, and every airliner follows it. Each engine feeds its own side of a common manifold through its bleed valves; pneumatic ducts are drawn dashed. A crossbleed, or isolation, valve in the middle can join or separate the two sides. The APU joins the manifold on one side, through its own bleed valve and a check valve that prevents reverse flow into it. And the users are connected along the manifold: the air conditioning packs, the wing anti-ice, the engine starters and the pressurisation of other systems. The point of the architecture is flexibility with isolation: any source can feed any user, and a failed or leaking side can be cut off.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slide 6 (generic scheme)

6. Where the bleed air comes from

2 · Bleed air
  • Normal: INTERMEDIATE stage (A320: HP stage 5) through a check valve (IPCV)
  • Idle / low thrust: HIGH stage (HP stage 9) through the HP valve (HPV)
  • Both join at the pressure regulating valve (PRV, 44 psi)
  • Precooler: hot bleed cooled by FAN air, no mixing; fan air regulated by the fan air valve (FAV)
  • Pressure and temperature sensors let the computers monitor and control everything
Narration

Now the heart of the system: how the air is taken from the engine. Normally the air comes from an intermediate compressor stage, the fifth stage of the high pressure compressor on the A320, through a check valve, the intermediate pressure check valve, that lets it flow only outwards. At idle or low thrust, stage 5 cannot give enough pressure, so the air is taken from a higher stage, stage 9, through the high pressure valve, the HPV. Both paths join upstream of the pressure regulating valve, the PRV, which delivers 44 psi to the system. The air is then cooled in the precooler, a heat exchanger where cold air bled from the fan flows around the tubes carrying the hot bleed air; the two streams never mix, and the used fan air is dumped overboard; a fan air valve controls how much fan air is used. Pressure and temperature sensors at several points let the bleed computers monitor and control the whole chain.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slide 7; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.2

7. A typical bleed regulating valve

2 · Bleed air
  • Butterfly in the duct, moved by a PNEUMATIC actuator; ELECTRICALLY controlled (solenoid)
  • Actuator compares a reference pressure with the downstream pressure: more downstream → close
  • Functions: switch the bleed ON/OFF, REGULATE pressure, PROTECT against over-temperature and reverse flow
  • Position switches + built-in test (BIT) confirm where the butterfly is
Narration

The deck shows the typical bleed regulating valve, and the A320 valves follow the same pattern. A butterfly in the duct is moved by a pneumatic actuator, while the valve is controlled electrically through a solenoid: electrically controlled, pneumatically actuated. The actuator compares a reference pressure with the pressure downstream of the butterfly: if the downstream pressure is too high it closes the butterfly a little, and if it is too low it opens it, so the butterfly hunts constantly around the reference value. The valve therefore has three functions: to switch the bleed on and off, to regulate the pressure, and to protect the system against high temperature and reverse flow. Position switches send discrete signals which, together with the pressures, are checked by a built-in test to confirm the real position of the butterfly.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slide 8; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.2.1

8. The users of bleed air

3 · Users
  • Wing anti-ice: valve per wing; perforated 'piccolo' duct bathes the leading edge; telescopic ducts to the slats (from slat 3); over-pressure/over-temperature sensors
  • Engine anti-ice: own valves on the fan case send hot air to the intake cowl; pressure switch confirms operation
  • Engine start: air from APU, ground cart or other engine → START valve → air starter on the gearbox → N2, then N1 (crank without fuel)
  • Hydraulic reservoirs: 50 psi; normally from HP stage 8 of ENGINE 1 (the deck drawing says 9: wrong)
  • Potable water tank, aft cargo heating, packs (ATA 21)
Narration

Let us look at each user in turn. Wing anti-ice sends hot bleed air through a valve on each wing into a perforated duct along the leading edge, which bathes its inside surface; telescopic ducts with flexible joints carry the air to the slats, from slat 3 outwards, and over-pressure and over-temperature sensors let the valve cut the supply if something goes wrong. Engine anti-ice is separate: one or more valves on the fan case send hot air from the engine itself to the intake cowl, and a pressure switch tells the crew whether it is working. For engine start, air from the APU, the ground cart or the other engine reaches the starter valve, which opens and drives the air starter on the accessory gearbox: the starter turns the high pressure spool, N2, which drags the fan, N1, and fuel is injected once N2 has reached the right speed; turning the engine without fuel is called cranking. The hydraulic reservoirs are pressurised to 50 psi, normally from stage 8 of the high pressure compressor of engine 1; the professor insists that the deck drawing, which shows stage 9, is wrong. Finally the potable water tank, the aft cargo heating and, above all, the air conditioning packs, which belong to chapter 21.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 9–12; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.3–1.4 (stage 8, not 9)

9. The A320 system at a glance

4 · A320 architecture
  • Sources: ENG 1, ENG 2, APU, ground connection
  • Crossbleed valve separates the two sides; when CLOSED each engine feeds its own pack
  • Engine 1 also feeds the aft cargo heating
  • APU bleed has PRIORITY: selecting it closes both engine bleed valves
  • APU and ground cart feed the LEFT side of the crossbleed duct
Narration

Here is the A320 layout with its users. Air is obtained from the two engines, the APU, or a ground cart, selected with the ENG 1 BLEED, ENG 2 BLEED and APU BLEED pushbuttons. A crossbleed valve in the transverse duct separates the two sides or joins them so that a single source feeds every user; when it is closed, each engine feeds its own air conditioning pack. On the deck's drawing, engine 1 also feeds the heating of the aft cargo compartment. The system is designed so that the APU bleed has priority: as soon as APU BLEED is selected, the APU bleed valve opens and both engine bleed valves close automatically, so the APU supplies everything. And remember that the APU and the ground connection both enter on the left side, side 1, of the crossbleed duct.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 13–14; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.4

10. The overhead panel: bleed pushbuttons and X-BLEED

4 · A320 architecture
  • Panel 21VU, shared by ATA 21 and ATA 36
  • ENG 1/2 BLEED: no light = bleed requested; white OFF = deselected; amber FAULT = failure (+ ECAM)
  • APU BLEED: normally dark; blue ON when selected; amber FAULT on failure
  • X-BLEED selector AUTO / OPEN / SHUT: two electric motors (one AUTO, one manual)
  • AUTO: the X-BLEED valve follows the APU bleed valve (opens only when bleeding from the APU)
  • Dark-cockpit philosophy: a light means something abnormal or a non-standard selection
Narration

The crew controls the system from the overhead panel 21VU, which the air conditioning and the pneumatic systems share. Each engine bleed pushbutton has two legends: with the bleed requested it is dark, a white OFF appears when the crew deselects it, and an amber FAULT, together with an ECAM message, signals a failure on that side. The APU BLEED pushbutton is normally dark, shows a blue ON when selected, and an amber FAULT on failure. The crossbleed valve is commanded by a rotary selector with three positions, AUTO, OPEN and SHUT, and the valve has two electric motors, one used by the automatic logic and the other by the manual positions. In AUTO the crossbleed valve always follows the APU bleed valve: it opens only when air is taken from the APU, while OPEN and SHUT force it regardless. This is the Airbus dark cockpit: in normal operation the panel is dark, and any light means an abnormal condition or a non-standard selection that must eventually be cancelled.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 15–16; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.4 (panel 21VU)

11. ECAM: how the system is shown to the crew

4 · A320 architecture
  • Upper ECAM, Engine/Warning display: N1, N2, EGT, fuel flow, fuel on board, slats/flaps; warnings at the bottom
  • Class 1 RED warnings: immediate crew action; class 2 AMBER cautions: no immediate action
  • Class 3: not shown to the crew, read by maintenance on the MCDU
  • Lower ECAM, System Display: BLEED page (pushbutton on the ECAM control panel)
  • BLEED page: valves, pressures, temperatures; 'GND' only on the ground
Narration

Information reaches the crew through the ECAM, the two central screens of the main instrument panel. The upper one, the Engine and Warning display, permanently shows the main engine parameters, N1, N2, exhaust gas temperature and fuel flow, plus the fuel on board and the high-lift surfaces, with the warning and caution messages at the bottom. Class 1 alerts are red warnings that require immediate crew action, and class 2 are amber cautions that do not. Class 3 failures are not shown to the crew at all: maintenance reads them on the multipurpose control and display unit, the MCDU. The lower screen, the System Display, shows synoptic pages, chosen automatically by flight phase or with the pushbuttons of the ECAM control panel; the BLEED pushbutton brings up this page. It shows the position of every bleed valve, the pressures and temperatures, and a GND indication that appears only when the aircraft is on the ground, detected by the landing gear proximity sensors.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slide 19; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.4 (ECAM, figure 6)

13. The brains: BMCs and the computers around them

4 · A320 architecture
  • BMC 1 and BMC 2 (identical, interchangeable): left and right side, linked by data buses
  • Inputs: pushbuttons, X-BLEED, fire pushbuttons, valve positions, pressure transmitters (Pt, Pr), temperature sensor, leak loops
  • Actions: supervise, close the bleed of their side on failure, inform crew (ECAM) and maintenance
  • Around them: SDAC → FWC (alerts) + DMC (displays); CFDIU (fault centralisation); EIU ← ECU (engine data); ECB (APU)
  • Leak-detection loops along the ducts and pylons → BMC isolates the leaking side
Narration

The system is supervised by two Bleed Monitoring Computers, BMC 1 and BMC 2, identical and interchangeable, one for each side, permanently connected to each other by data buses. Each receives electrical signals from the pushbuttons, the crossbleed valve, the engine and APU fire pushbuttons, the positions of the bleed valves, the two pressure transmitters, transferred pressure before the PRV and regulated pressure after it, the temperature sensor and the leak detection loops of its side, and some of the other side. With them it supervises the bleed, shuts off its side if it detects a failure, and informs the crew through the ECAM and maintenance through the fault system. Around them, the System Data Acquisition Concentrators, the SDACs, collect the data for the Flight Warning Computers, which generate the aural and visual alerts, and for the three display computers, the DMCs, two working and one in standby; the CFDIU centralises the faults of every system; the Engine Interface Units pass on the data that each engine's own control unit collects; and the APU is controlled by its Electronic Control Box, the ECB. Finally, leak detection loops run alongside the ducts, the pylons and the APU duct; when one detects hot air, the BMC isolates the leak by closing the corresponding bleed valve before nearby components are damaged.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 17–19; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.4 (figures 4–5)

14. APU bleed logic and the ground connection

5 · APU bleed
  • APU feeds the LEFT side of the crossbleed duct, on ground and in flight up to 20 000 ft
  • APU BLEED valve: pneumatic actuator, SOLENOID controlled; plus a check valve against reverse flow
  • Press APU BLEED → BMC checks: no APU fire pushbutton, no APU-duct leak (leaks tolerated on the ground until the engines start)
  • BMC → ECB; if no APU shut-down signal and APU speed stabilised at 95 % → bleed valve opens
  • Ground cart: access panel lower LEFT fuselage, connector with a two-flap check valve
Narration

The APU bleed has its own logic, shown in this diagram. The APU supplies air directly to the left side of the crossbleed duct, on the ground and in flight up to 20 000 feet. Its bleed valve is operated by a pneumatic actuator and controlled by a solenoid, and a check valve downstream prevents air flowing back into the APU. When the crew presses APU BLEED, the blue ON light comes on and the BMC checks that the APU fire pushbutton has not been operated and that no leak is detected in the APU duct; on the ground a leak is tolerated until the engines have started, after which the APU has to be shut down. If those conditions are met, the BMC signals the APU's Electronic Control Box, which opens the bleed valve provided there is no APU shut-down signal and the APU speed is stabilised at 95 %. The ground cart, finally, connects through a quick-release access panel on the lower left fuselage to a connector whose check valve has two spring-loaded flaps that open only inwards.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 20–21; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.4 (figures 7–8)

15. Starting the engines with APU air

5 · APU bleed
  • Engine stopped → no engine bleed: use APU (or ground cart)
  • APU BLEED ON → APU valve opens; with X-BLEED in AUTO the crossbleed valve OPENS too
  • Air flows 'backwards' along the duct to each START valve → air starter → N2 → N1
  • Start sequence of an engine → its BMC closes that engine's PRV (no bleed during start)
  • After start: starter valve closes; engine bleed restored when APU BLEED is deselected (PRVs reopen and regulate)
  • Leak during the start sequence: X-BLEED stays open until the sequence ends
Narration

Starting the engines puts all of this together, and it is the subject of the first question of the 2020 quiz. With the engines stopped there is no engine bleed, so the air comes from the APU or a ground cart. Selecting APU BLEED opens the APU bleed valve and, with the crossbleed selector in AUTO, the crossbleed valve follows it and opens, so APU air reaches both sides. The air then flows in the opposite direction to normal, from the manifold towards the engine, up to the start valve, which opens and feeds the air starter, which turns N2 and then N1. During the start sequence of an engine, its BMC keeps that engine's pressure regulating valve closed electrically, so the starting engine does not bleed. Once the engine is running, the start valve closes; when the crew deselects APU BLEED, the engine pressure regulating valves reopen and regulate again. And if a leak is detected during the start sequence, the crossbleed valve stays open until the sequence ends, so the start is not interrupted; so yes, both engines can be started with APU bleed with the selector in AUTO.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022, slides 14–16, 24, 34; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic), §1.4, §1.4.2; Cuestionario de sistemas, Nov 2020 (compartit/Examenes) (question 1)

16. True or false? Eight quick statements

Practice

Self-test: think of your answer, then open it.

1 · The APU can supply bleed air in flight at any altitude

FALSE: up to 20 000 ft

2 · APU and ground cart feed the RIGHT side of the crossbleed duct

FALSE: the LEFT side (1)

3 · Selecting APU BLEED closes the engine bleed valves automatically

TRUE: APU priority

4 · In AUTO the X-BLEED opens whenever an engine bleed fails

FALSE: it only follows the APU valve; crew selects OPEN

5 · Hydraulic reservoirs are pressurised from HP stage 9 of engine 2

FALSE: stage 8 of ENGINE 1

6 · Class 3 failures appear in amber on the upper ECAM

FALSE: maintenance only (MCDU)

7 · Each BMC supervises its side and gets some signals from the other side

TRUE

8 · Engine anti-ice takes air from the wing anti-ice duct

FALSE: own valves, own engine, intake cowl

Narration

Time to practise in the exam format: after each statement, pause the video, decide true or false, and then listen. One: the APU can supply bleed air in flight at any altitude. False: its bleed is limited to 20 000 feet. Two: the APU and the ground cart feed the right side of the crossbleed duct. False: both feed the left side, side 1. Three: selecting APU BLEED closes the engine bleed valves automatically. True: that is the APU priority. Four: in AUTO, the crossbleed valve opens whenever an engine bleed fails. False: in AUTO it only follows the APU bleed valve, and the crew must select OPEN to cross-feed from the other engine. Five: the hydraulic reservoirs are pressurised from stage 9 of engine 2. False: from stage 8 of engine 1, with the pneumatic manifold as a backup. Six: class 3 failures appear in amber on the upper ECAM. False: amber is class 2, and class 3 is only for maintenance, on the MCDU. Seven: each BMC supervises its own side and also receives some signals from the other side. True. Eight: the engine anti-ice takes its air from the wing anti-ice duct. False: each engine has its own anti-ice valves on the fan case, feeding its intake cowl with its own bleed.

Sources: Esbrí, Sistema neumático (ATA 36), course deck 2022; Comunitat del Overleaf, Apunts vehicles aeroespacials §1 (pneumatic)

17. Part 1 in one slide

Summary
  • Sources: engines (stage 5, stage 9 at idle), APU (left side, ≤ 20 000 ft), ground cart (left side)
  • Users: packs, engine start, wing/engine anti-ice, hydraulic reservoirs (stage 8, ENG 1), water, aft cargo heat
  • Regulation chain: IPCV / HPV → PRV → OPV → precooler (FAV + TCT)
  • APU bleed has priority; X-BLEED in AUTO follows the APU bleed valve
  • BMC 1/2 supervise and isolate; ECAM shows; dark cockpit
44 psiPRV regulation36 psiHPV regulation200 °Cprecooler outlet
Narration

To summarise part one: air comes from stage 5 of the engines, or stage 9 at idle, from the APU up to 20 000 feet, or from a ground cart, and the APU and the cart feed the left side. It goes to the packs, the engine starters, wing and engine anti-ice, the hydraulic reservoirs, pressurised from stage 8 of engine 1, the water tank and the aft cargo heating. On its way it passes the check valve or the high pressure valve, the pressure regulating valve, the overpressure valve and the precooler with its fan air valve and temperature control thermostat. The APU bleed has priority over the engines, and the crossbleed valve in AUTO follows the APU bleed valve. Two BMCs supervise and isolate, the ECAM shows the state, and the cockpit stays dark when all is well; in part two we open every valve and learn its exact logic and numbers.
