The 2021 final also asks about Units 3–4 (antennas, radar, SATCOM, mixers, amplifiers, the labs), whose slides are not on Atenea yet, so the videos stop at matching. These notes give the standard relations those questions need, formula first.

Frequency bands: radar letters, SATCOM, weather radar

IEEE bands:L 1–2, S 2–4, C 4–8, X 8–12, Ku 12–18, Ka 27–40 GHz

Aviation SATCOM (Inmarsat): aircraft ↔ satellite in L band (1.5–1.66 GHz); satellite ↔ ground station feeder links in C band (uplink 6 GHz, downlink 4 GHz); broadband in Ku/Ka. A geostationary satellite is a repeater (“bent pipe”) for many channels at once and needs line of sight to both ends. Primary ATC radar: S band (2.7–2.9 GHz). Weather radar: C band (≈ 5.6 GHz, ground) and X band (≈ 9.4 GHz, airborne): X gives a narrower beam from the same dish (better resolution) but is attenuated more by rain.

Lumped resonators, filters, diplexers and duplexers

f0​=2πLC​1​
lumped if the component size≪λ (≲λ/10); otherwise distributed (line sections)

Each LC resonator (series or shunt) is one pole: three resonators make a 3rd-order (3-pole) band-pass filter. A diplexer is two filters on one common port that separate two frequency bands: the FDM receiver splits channels with it. A duplexer lets a transmitter and a receiver share one antenna (radar: T/R switch or circulator, then a limiter that protects the receiver).

Antenna gain, beamwidth and effective area

G=ηD,G≈θE​θH​4π​ (rad)≈θE∘​θH∘​41253​
Ae​=4πGλ2​
dish: G=η(λπd​)2,θ−3dB​≈70dλ​ degrees

A narrower beam means more gain. Read the −3 dB beamwidth between the two points of a pattern that sit 3 dB below the maximum. TASN part 3 (antennas) uses the same D, Aₑ and Friis relations.

Pulse radar: ranges, resolution, sensitivity

R=2cΔt​,Run​=2cTr​​=2fr​c​
Rmin​=ΔR=2cτ​
Pr​=(4π)3R4Pt​G2λ2σ​,DR=P1dB​−Smin​

Δt is the echo delay; the factor 2 is the round trip. An echo arriving after the next pulse looks closer than it is (range ambiguity). A datasheet “sensitivity” is the minimum detectable power, so any weaker signal is not detected; a longer pulse puts more energy on target and improves it.

Aviation VHF voice: channel spacing

N=Δffmax​−fmin​​:25 kHz137−118 MHz​=760,8.33 kHz19 MHz​=2280

VHF voice uses AM, DSB, between 118 and 137 MHz; 108–117.975 MHz is for VOR/ILS. Europe moved from 25 kHz to 8.33 kHz spacing to triple the number of channels. Over-modulation widens the spectrum and causes adjacent-channel interference.

Mixers: conversion gain, isolation, leakage

fIF​=∣fRF​−fLO​∣,fimage​=fLO​±fIF​ (the other side of the LO)
CG [dB]=PIF​(fIF​)−PRF​(fRF​),isolationLO–IF​=PLO​−PIFport​(fLO​)

Take each power at its own port and frequency. A negative CG is a conversion loss: passive diode mixers lose ≈ 6 dB, active ones can have gain. The LO line that shows up at the RF or IF port is leakage, not signal.

Antenna bandwidth and automatic tuners

SWR≤2⇔∣Γ∣≤31​⇔Pin​Pr​​≤11 %
L=ωX​, C=ωB​⇒L,C∝f1​

The antenna's bandwidth is the frequency range where its SWR stays below the radio's limit (usually 2). Outside it an automatic tuner, an L/C network set by an SWR-detector feedback loop, re-matches the antenna. A fixed L-section matches one frequency only, and its elements scale as 1/f.

S-parameters themselves are on the RF 3 page (scattering parameters). With 50 Ω terminations only |S₂₁| matters; S₁₁ and S₂₂ tell you how much better a matched design could do.

vp​=Δt2l​,εr​=(vp​c​)2

A step Eᵢ travels down the line; the load sends back Γ·Eᵢ after the round-trip time Δt. Open: the step doubles (Γ = +1); short: it falls to zero (Γ = −1); R > Z₀ steps up, R < Z₀ steps down; an inductive load gives an upward spike, a capacitive one a dip. Every impedance change along the cable adds its own step, and multiple reflections bounce back and forth, attenuated by the product of the Γs.

Image rejection and SAW filters (lab 1)

fimage​=fRF​±2fIF​ (mirror of fRF​ about fLO​)

The image must be removed before the mixer, by the RF (preselector) filter, often a SAW filter in GPS front ends. After the mixer the image already sits on f_IF, so the IF filter can no longer separate it: the IF filter only selects the channel.

Microstrip patch antenna (lab 5)

L≈2εeff​​λ0​​,εeff​ depends on εr​ and the substrate height h

A metal patch over a ground plane. It radiates broadside, away from the ground plane, so it is directive (≈ 6–9 dBi), not omnidirectional. It is thin and cheap; with circular polarisation (RHCP) it is the usual GPS antenna.
